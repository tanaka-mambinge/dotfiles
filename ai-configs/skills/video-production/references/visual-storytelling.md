# Visual storytelling

Use references to make concrete creative choices. Start with supplied references and assets, then inspect relevant examples from the existing video workflows when useful. Borrow specific choices such as shot scale, composition, rhythm, transitions, texture, or typography; do not copy a reference wholesale or let reference style override the brief.

## Reveal with the narration

- Let voiceover or audio carry the full explanation. The image should add meaning, evidence, emotion, or context rather than repeat every spoken sentence as text.
- Reveal one idea at a time as it is spoken. Bring in a visual, detail, label, or keyword when the narration reaches it; let it land before introducing the next idea.
- Give the viewer a clear focal point. Avoid showing a full list of upcoming points or a finished information layout before the narration gets there.
- Use on-screen words selectively: a short hook, a key phrase, a number, a name, or a brief label. Marketing and promo videos should be especially text-light. Explainers can use more text when it makes a process, relationship, or unfamiliar term easier to understand.
- Use transcript timestamps to shape scene changes, pauses, keyword emphasis, and visual entrances. Listen to the audio for final timing, especially when a word needs precise emphasis; generated word timings are estimates.

## Make it feel like a video

Do not default to the visual language of a website, mobile app, slideshow, or presentation. Prefer designed shots, meaningful movement, changing scale, evolving compositions, footage, objects, graphics, and visual metaphors suited to the subject. Use interface imagery only when the product or idea calls for it, and keep it in service of the video rather than presenting a static screen of content.

## Text legibility

Keep every on-screen word readable against the actual background for the full time it appears. Check the text over footage, effects, and animated or changing backgrounds, including the brightest and darkest moments beneath it. If contrast is weak, adjust the text color or placement, or add a subtle backing that fits the scene. For HyperFrames work, use the available contrast check as an additional verification. Follow the user's direction if they explicitly ask for text that is difficult to read.
